# Slide layout system

Translate the reference into presentation grammar. Keep identity invariants stable: palette roles, typography, logo treatment, and recurring graphic language. Vary composition to support meaning: image scale, text scale, density, alignment, and whitespace.

Set canvas dimensions and a safe content area. Define measurable regions for headings, body, imagery, citations, and page numbers. Use a consistent type scale, but adjust text regions to their purpose. Word widths, font metrics, line spacing, and wrapping determine fit; character count alone does not.

| Purpose | Possible composition | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Opening | Brand signature or image with dominant title | Establishing topic and supplied author/date |
| Topic cover | Short title with section label, color field, or image | A meaningful topic change fits the slide budget |
| Explanation | Text beside an image or diagram | One idea needs elaboration |
| Editorial | Large image and large title in separate regions | A visual context or thesis deserves emphasis |
| Impact | Full image with a short statement in a protected reading area | Introducing an insight or transition |
| Typographic | Large statement on a brand-colored surface | The reference permits a text-led emphasis |
| Comparison | Aligned columns/rows with shared criteria | Comparing options fairly |
| Data | Dominant metric/chart, context, and source | Supplied or verified evidence supports it |
| Process | Ordered stages or connected steps | Sequence or causality is the message |
| Closing | Synthesis, question, or next step | Completing the narrative |

This is a repertoire, not a required sequence. Do not force photographic slides, red backgrounds, serif type, or another institution's signature onto every brand. Vary the deck according to meaning rather than mechanical alternation.

## Topic covers and rhythm

Reserve covers for major sections when the total allows. They count as slides. Preserve strict outline-to-slide correspondence; integrate a topic heading into a content slide when a separate cover would displace required content. Keep section numbering separate from global slide numbering.

Mix explanatory content with evidence, examples, and conclusions. Do not decorate every slide with a photo. For full-image layouts, protect the entire reading area without unnecessarily hiding the subject.

## Overflow repair

1. Remove redundancy while preserving claims, numbers, and qualifications.
2. Choose a roomier layout or redistribute text and imagery.
3. Adjust spacing and scale moderately while keeping legibility.
4. Split only if the requested slide count may change.

Never silently crop content or solve overflow with tiny type. Gather all issues for one slide and repair it together. Cosmetic preferences should guide targeted improvements rather than discard a valid batch.

Inspect the final exported file. Font substitutions, image crops, lost gradient overlays, SVG differences, and layer changes can invalidate a good source preview. Disclose any rasterized regions that limit editability.
