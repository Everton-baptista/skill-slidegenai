# Style grammar examples

Use when a user asks for a recognizable named direction from the SlideGenAI catalog or a repertoire of different treatments. Resolve the source entry before composing: `find_templates.py --name "NOVA"`. The source palette and preview establish a starting reference; the user's brand and fidelity requirements still control the work. These are optional composition recipes, not nine additional rendering engines or official brand masters.

These nine examples illustrate [the construction system shared by all 177 styles](all-template-construction.md). A missing named recipe never limits a template to a cover, generic fonts or fewer native component types.

## Translate character into relationships

A source preview may combine image placement, typography, material, density, light and sequence. Retain a few observable relationships and adapt them to the content. Record what is observed versus a new creative choice. Do not flatten an entire slide into an image to get an expressive result. Image-generated artwork can carry texture, sculpture or atmosphere; native type and quantitative evidence remain separate.

For a new direction, choose a representative cover and evidence page. Test that both belong to the same visual language. A strong cover followed by a generic table dump does not establish a working presentation system. Data may need a quieter surface while retaining the type hierarchy, semantic colors and aligned labels.

## Acid Rave / Acid Graphix (`acid-graphix`)

**Observed starting point:** black and acid lime, Y2K rave, chrome and distorted texture. The visible poster label is Acid Rave.

- Cover: a compressed oversized live headline against a deliberately irregular raster artwork; allow the image to occupy the opposite visual mass rather than putting every title in the same left gradient frame.
- Content: a photograph with a blunt typographic proposition, offset blocks of aligned text and short monospaced event information when useful.
- Topic pause: a hard lime reading field or a typographic poster, with a different scale from the cover.
- Evidence: keep exact native bar lengths, real category order and explicit fictional-data labels; texture lives outside the plot.
- Closing: a large declarative typographic composition can end the deck without the standard dark institutional “next step” page.

Avoid turning the style into a generic neon dashboard, distorting axes for a rave effect, adding drug imagery by association, or hiding words in an unreadable glitch.

## Neo-Tech (`default`)

**Observed starting point:** near-black, pale gray and lime; precise high contrast. The canonical ID is `default`, not `neo-tech`.

- Cover: quiet engineered imagery with a clean, large product name and a small functional descriptor.
- Content: a meaningful count, state or relationship, with stable alignment and ample space around the proposition.
- Topic pause: restrained live type and one active accent, not mandatory photographic drama.
- Evidence: native comparisons with consistent scales and labels; line work expresses actual architecture or responsibility.
- Closing: an open light field or compact action statement can preserve precision without repeating the same dark cover.

Avoid importing Cyberpunk's visual noise, fake terminal code, invented KPI cards, or decorative circuit connections.

## Cyberpunk (`cyberpunk`)

**Observed starting point:** near-black, hot pink and cyan, technological night imagery and controlled glitch.

- Cover: a hard-edged headline in tension with physical image atmosphere. Small offset live text echoes may express a title treatment only while the primary words stay unambiguous.
- Content: image-led darkness with protected reading regions; sparse technical annotation should convey real content.
- Topic pause: a sharp color interruption can reset the pace.
- Evidence: a calm plot region with differentiated cyan/pink series, explicit units and real x/y data for scatter.
- Closing: a pale or white page with dark type can make a deliberate visual break while preserving the type and accent relationships.

Avoid invented code, faux security badges, distorted numbers, or making every slide an alley photograph. Fictional futuristic scenes must not be presented as real events.

## NOVA / Fashion Editorial (`fashion-editorial`)

**Observed starting point:** monochrome fashion photography, strong red live text, white space and yellow accents; split editorial construction. NOVA is the preview label.

- Cover: use the image edge and white reading field as part of the composition; a large red title may align across the boundary if faces and required detail remain visible.
- Content: preserve the photographed silhouette; position text in its actual negative space instead of applying the same dark gradient as a technology deck.
- Topic pause: a typographic spread, offset title or red field can function like a new editorial chapter.
- Evidence: a native collection table or a small categorical allocation with the same hierarchy and restrained rules.
- Closing: a specific photographic crop with a short live red proposition; no generic corporate CTA is required.

Avoid arbitrary claims about real designers, fabricated quotes, fake publication metadata, or using a supplied person's image as a new identity without authorization.

## Ultimate Luxury / Premium Escapes (`premium-travel`)

**Observed starting point:** deep navy, warm gold and immersive resort photography. The source preview includes a third-party resort name; that name is not a reusable affiliation or asset.

- Cover: a quiet horizon and generous image space; serif hierarchy can be a creative adaptation when the supplied identity permits it.
- Content: use material, light and negative space to carry atmosphere. A smaller, well-spaced headline may be stronger than maximum type size.
- Topic pause: ivory and dark navy offer a calm transition; gold supports hierarchy rather than covering every surface.
- Evidence: readable service distinctions or an experience sequence. Do not invent revenue, occupancy, rankings or guest testimonials to fill a chart.
- Closing: an image band, a measured serif proposition or an almost-empty reading field can preserve restraint.

Avoid mandatory chrome effects, decorative gold stars, tiny low-contrast captions and real hotel affiliation claims.

## Neo Tokyo / Urban Vivid (`urban-vivid`)

**Observed starting point:** urban night photography, electric light trails, cyan/pink and deep black. Neo Tokyo is the preview label.

- Cover: let the photographed city set the anchor; a right- or bottom-aligned headline may better preserve the street than the default left title.
- Content: a broad urban crop with short observational text, contrasted by a quiet white reading page when the narrative needs a pause.
- Topic pause: an image window or split type field can imply an urban interval without inventing signage.
- Evidence: a native time series with clearly labeled hours; a semantic route diagram must be labeled conceptual rather than passed off as a real map.
- Closing: keep a specific nocturnal image and let the title inhabit its real open area.

Avoid fake Japanese lettering, fabricated place facts, readable generated signs, or collapsing the direction into Cyberpunk machinery. Do not claim observations about a real city from generated imagery.

## Bauhaus (`bauhaus`)

**Observed starting point:** warm off-white, red/blue/yellow, geometry and a strong organizing grid.

- Cover: oversized black type balanced against primary-color artwork or photographed objects; the grid governs unequal visual weights.
- Content: a material study or image/text counterweight, rather than mandatory full-bleed photography.
- Topic pause: use the type/grid relationship to mark a chapter; a number is useful only when it describes actual sequence.
- Evidence: native primary-color marks with exact magnitudes and dark readable labels. A yellow bar needs dark labels or labels outside it.
- Closing: a light, asymmetric typographic poster can retain the grammar without the same dark ending as other decks.

Decorative geometric artwork is still artwork: use an appropriate sourced/generated asset where the host requires it. Required charts/diagrams remain native semantic objects. Do not invent a historical attribution or represent this adaptation as an official Bauhaus institutional template.

## Kinetic (`kinetic`)

**Observed starting point:** massive repeated type, yellow/black and strong energy. MOVE is the visible preview word; a static slide does not prove an animation is implemented.

- Cover: repeat a small number of live headline words with measured offsets and changing weight or color; protect the primary reading instead of clipping essential words.
- Content: use real photographic motion as a counterweight to stationary text; not every page needs repeated lettering.
- Topic pause: a large open type field can create an abrupt change in rhythm.
- Evidence: integer tick marks for counts, exact time widths for an editable schedule, clear labels outside motion imagery.
- Closing: a strong yellow/black typographic page may end the argument with a different geometry from the opening.

Avoid claiming exported motion from a static preview, decorative speed lines made to look like data, or reducing all text to a texture.

## Corporate & Education (category)

**Observed starting point:** this is a source category, not a single template ID. It includes institution-specific references; `education-modern` is a separate education direction. Choose the relevant source entry explicitly and preserve actual institutional identity when supplied.

- Cover: establish the program, intended audience and institution using evidenced or explicitly fictional identity.
- Content: connect a learning activity to its purpose and output; avoid empty promotional claims about outcomes.
- Topic pause: mark a real transition in the curriculum while counting it within the requested slide total.
- Evidence: keep curriculum tables, assessment criteria, feedback loops and requested diagrams editable.
- Closing: a concrete question, next activity or learner artifact can be more useful than the same corporate “next steps” template.

Do not borrow another institution's logo, seal or claimed official master. Do not invent learning gains or turn participation counts into proof of learning.

## Cross-deck review

Compare the source promise with the final artifacts, not merely their prompts. Inspect cover, content, chapter, evidence and closing pages at reading size and the full overview for rhythm. Ask which structural relationship makes each direction recognizable without its palette. Record repeated compositions and deliberate recurrence separately; changing every slide for novelty can also weaken a coherent deck. Keep exact templates and controlled comparisons stable when the user requires them.

The source catalog remains a 177-entry metadata inventory. A demonstrated local artifact establishes only its recorded construction/render path and scope. It does not certify every template, host, font, image generator or exporter.

## Typography

Use the role-based [typography guide](typography.md) and optional [pairing catalog](typography-pairings.json) when selecting fonts or modernizing a direction. Preserve evidenced typography in faithful mode; verify the actual host fonts, reflow changed text, and test native outline transparency and contextual tags.
