# Repertoire review: composition beyond palettes

## Scope and evidence

This review concerns two local fictional demonstration batches requested by the user, not an independent ranking of presentation tools. The first batch contained eight Portuguese presentations with eight slides each. The source plans, native scene objects and final 1280 × 720 renders were inspected in Codex desktop on macOS using live Artifact Sessions. All brand identities, scenarios and numerical examples were fictional. No external institution's logo or official master was redistributed.

## What worked in the first eight decks

The individual presentations kept a coherent palette, readable type hierarchy and useful narrative. Photography, live reading gradients and live text were separate objects. Eleven native charts retained their source values, including actual scatter x/y pairs; eight comparison/curriculum tables remained native. Processes and feedback paths used attached connector objects. All 64 final slides and each overview were inspected, and the live native chart values were compared with the source data.

Repairs mattered: light green flow labels needed darker text; chart outlines needed to match series fills; count axes needed integer tick marks; a schedule needed widths that matched its stated times; an edge label crossed a connector; a feedback path contained an avoidable jog. These were concrete construction issues, not a reason to claim the entire first batch was unusable.

## What limited the demonstrated range

Seven covers used immersive photography with a left reading region; the magazine direction used a white split field. All eight closings used a dark background, a large white title and a bottom next-step line. Their largest closing text frames were identical: `(60, 198, 1150, 350)` on a 1280 × 720 canvas. This consistency worked within individual projects but narrowed a showcase intended to demonstrate different visual directions.

Several source labels were also hard to discover: Acid Rave appeared under Acid Graphix, NOVA under Fashion Editorial, Ultimate Luxury under Premium Escapes and Neo Tokyo under Urban Vivid. Searching only titles/IDs hid useful styles even though they were already in the 177-entry catalog. Corporate & Education is a category; it must not become a fabricated template ID.

The earlier family-level recipes were too broad to describe the distinction between a restrained technology presentation, Cyberpunk and an urban night essay. Shared color accents do not establish equivalent image roles, typography or rhythm.

## Changes made to the portable skill

- Added source-observed preview labels and exact normalized `--name` lookup. Canonical IDs remain unchanged; ambiguity returns every match, and misspellings are not silently guessed.
- Added nine composition-level [style grammars](../brand-presentations/references/style-grammars.md), including evidence pages and closings rather than cover-only descriptions.
- Added a cross-deck critique to art direction: compare title placement, image geometry, density, section and closing behavior independently of palette. Preserve deliberate repeated frames for exact templates and controlled comparisons.
- Kept data and semantic geometry strict: visual texture does not distort charts, and static kinetic typography does not certify exported animation.

## Second local study

The follow-up study uses the requested Acid Rave, Neo-Tech, Cyberpunk, NOVA, Ultimate Luxury, Neo Tokyo, Bauhaus, Kinetic and Corporate & Education directions. The user clarified that “batth” meant Bauhaus. Each project is a fictional six-slide presentation, with its content and geometry separated from reusable style guidance.

The intended comparisons are explicit: Cyberpunk uses a physical technological atmosphere and restrained title echoes, while Neo Tokyo uses urban photographic observation; luxury uses calm serif hierarchy, while Kinetic uses repeated live type; NOVA uses monochrome fashion and red on white, while Bauhaus uses a primary-color art/grid relationship. Evidence objects are selected by the content, not inserted to satisfy a universal chart quota.

## Observed results of the second study

All nine live decks were completed with six slides each. All 54 final slides were rendered and individually inspected after the font refinement pass; each overview was also inspected. Seven native charts matched their declared numerical series, including actual scatter x/y coordinates. Four native tables retained every declared cell value. Twenty-four connector segments referenced endpoints on their own slide. The minute schedule retained proportional native bar geometry. The eighteen selected new raster assets were generated concept imagery, not documentary photographs.

## Typography feedback and changes

The user correctly identified that the initial implementation relied too heavily on Arial, Impact and Georgia. The final local pass uses seven display voices across nine decks, with twelve distinct families across display, body and label roles:

| Direction | Display | Body | Label |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Acid Rave | Impact | DIN Alternate | PT Mono |
| Neo-Tech | DIN Alternate | Avenir Next | Menlo |
| Cyberpunk | DIN Condensed | DIN Alternate | Menlo |
| NOVA | Bodoni 72 | Helvetica Neue | Helvetica Neue |
| Ultimate Luxury | Didot | Avenir Next | Optima |
| Neo Tokyo | DIN Condensed | Helvetica Neue | Menlo |
| Bauhaus | Futura | Avenir Next | Futura |
| Kinetic | Impact | Avenir Next | DIN Alternate |
| Corporate & Education example | Avenir Next | Seravek | Seravek |

These fonts were available on the tested Mac; no proprietary font binaries were copied into the repository. The Kinetic cover's middle word uses native transparent text fill and a native glyph outline, and it remained editable text. Neo-Tech and the urban essay use contextual native outlined labels. NOVA's existing label uses a native filled reading field. Body, table and chart labels retain readable solid text.

The portable skill now includes a dedicated typography decision guide and nine optional starting pairings using official Google Fonts specimen links. These suggestions are separate from the fonts actually rendered above. They were not automatically installed or certified in other hosts. The guide covers role selection, outline capability, label semantics, accents, font resolution, fallback reflow and separate export inspection.

## Remaining limits

The source-independent hierarchy is more varied, but the four-stage linear process remains a shared structure in several studies. It is appropriate to these particular narratives; a later analytical brief should select a different topology when its relationships require branching, feedback, lanes or dependency structure. More decorative complexity would not improve an inherently linear story.

This is a local qualitative study with direct native-object checks. It does not establish a numerical aesthetic score, comparison against every presentation product, animation support, font embedding, or successful native PowerPoint, Google Slides or Keynote editing. Other providers remain untested. The catalog remains source-reviewed metadata rather than 177 individually verified exporters.
